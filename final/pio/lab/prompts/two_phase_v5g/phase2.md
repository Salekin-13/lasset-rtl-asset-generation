THIS CALL: PHASE 2, STRUCTURAL ASSETS (POINTS OF INFLUENCE AND OBSERVATION)

You receive one more input: CONCEPTS, the "Conceptual Assets" list of Phase 1. Each entry has a "Concept ID" (C1, C2, ...), a short name ("Concept Name"), its "Unit", the "Information", the "Objectives At Risk" (C, I, A), the ports it "Enters Through" and "Leaves Through", and the RTL lines where it enters ("Entry Lines"), is stored or produced ("Held Lines") and leaves ("Exit Lines"). Its "Rubric", "Rubric Reason" and "Attack" are Phase 1's answers to Phase 1's questions: use them as context, not as answers to this call's questions, except as step 2 says.
Treat the list as fixed: analyse every conceptual asset in it, even one you doubt, and do not add, merge or rename any. If you think one is missing or wrong, say so in "Notes"; do not analyse a missing one.

Steps, for each conceptual asset in CONCEPTS:

1. Walk. Work in the diagram of the conceptual asset's unit.
   Start at the blocks that contain its held lines, and at its entry and exit ports (for a port field, at its port). A conceptual asset that is about a clock or a reset itself starts at the blocks whose notes name it and at the instance blocks its arrows reach (2.2). A held line that is a concurrent assignment to an output port outside every generate statement belongs to no block: start at that assignment (see "Points" below). One written inside a generate statement belongs to that [generate] block: start there.
   An arrow from A to B moves the conceptual asset when one of its listed elements carries it (2.2) into B: on a "==>" arrow by an explicit flow, on either kind by an index. It decides the conceptual asset when its listed elements only decide whether, when or where B stores, moves, gates, clears or releases it. Judge each listed element by what it does in B: read its closed-set counterparts whose cited lines are statements of B (B's members, and the output ports or port fields that B's own statements assign; for an [inst] block, B's port-map lines), and read those RTL lines. One element can carry different information on different lines, for example a bus data field that carries settings into one block and other data into another, so do not judge it by its uses elsewhere. The brackets can miss an element; the counterparts and the RTL show it. For an arrow from a block into an output port, with or without brackets, also judge the "gets value from" and "decided by" partners of the port, or of its fields for a record port, whose cited lines are statements of A, wherever those partners are assigned. An arrow from an [inst] block into an output port is a port-map write when it has no brackets or when its brackets name a port of the instantiated entity: for an instance of another IP the black-box rule decides; for an instance of this file, judge that port by its entry in the child entity's list.
   Forward: from an entry port or a holding block, follow the arrows that move the conceptual asset, and keep walking from each block they reach. An arrow whose elements carry the conceptual asset into a block that only tests them (a condition or a case selector) also reaches that block: list it as a point, but do not walk on from it.
   Backward: from an exit port or a holding block, follow backward the arrows that move it, and keep walking back from each block they come from. An arrow into a block on the walk makes its source block an influence point in two cases: its elements only decide the conceptual asset, or they are values from which the block computes the conceptual asset (an operand of an AND, an OR, a compare or a sum) without carrying it. List that block as a point, but do not walk back from it.
   Instances of another IP: when the conceptual asset enters one, every element the instance drives carries it (the black-box rule of section 1). Follow the arrows out of it that list those elements, and say in each point's "Answer Reason" that this is the assumption. Go one instance deep.
   Do not follow an arrow for a clock or reset it lists: it carries no value (2.2). Stop at the unit's ports, and where no arrow moves or decides the conceptual asset any further.
   Points: every block reached this way is a point. A concurrent assignment to an output port that belongs to no block (shown by an arrow with brackets into an output port from a [process], [generate] or [logic] block, or from an [inst] block whose brackets name a signal of the unit rather than a port of the instantiated entity, or by an arrow from an input port straight into one) is a point too; name it after that output port, as OUTPUTS writes it. The unit's ports themselves are where the walk starts and ends; they are not points.

2. Questions. For every point, answer the four questions below for this conceptual asset:
   Confidentiality (the point as an observation point). Does the point expose the conceptual asset: can its value be read out, or inferred, there?
   Integrity (the point as an influence point). Does the point allow any modification of the conceptual asset?
   Availability. Can the point prevent the conceptual asset from being available for functional operation?
   Undermined expected behavior. Does the point have a special behavior, such as a privileged mode, an override, a bypass or test-packet injection, that can prevent the conceptual asset from being available for normal operation? A special behavior that changes the conceptual asset itself is answered under integrity.
   Assume the question's objective as section 3 says. A question whose objective Phase 1 did not list for this conceptual asset is answered "no": confidentiality needs C, integrity needs I, availability and undermined expected behavior need A. If an entry lacks "Objectives At Risk", the list was left out, not emptied: rebuild it from its "Rubric", as Phase 1 does, and use it wherever this call names the objectives Phase 1 listed. A "yes" to confidentiality gives C, to integrity I, to availability A, and to undermined expected behavior the objective its "Rubric Reason" says the behavior breaks (I when it makes the output incorrect, A when it stops the function).
   A "yes" needs a mechanism that the RTL shows, or that the black-box rule gives; cite its line. Judge by 2.9: at a point that stores or sets the conceptual asset, follow its last bullet; at any other point, follow the bullet before it. When every answer is "no", say why.

3. Structural assets. At every point with at least one "yes", name the closed-set elements that store, carry, produce or control the conceptual asset there:
   - the members of the block that do so. At a block that is a point only because an arrow out of it reaches the walk (an influence point), these are the members that this arrow lists, and any the counterparts show the brackets missed. A member that acts on the conceptual asset only through another member is not named here;
   - the unit ports the block reads the conceptual asset from, and the output ports that its arrows reach with the conceptual asset;
   - at a concurrent assignment to an output port: the output port, or the field of it, that the assignment writes, and the input ports, or fields, it reads the conceptual asset from.
   Name the element that carries this conceptual asset: a record field when only that field carries it, even when the RTL also assigns or wires the whole record; the whole record or port only when every field it uses carries it. A unit port that only decides at this point (an enable, a strobe, an address) is not named here; step 5 checks it.
   When the point reads or writes the same field of several unit ports of one role (ports that differ only by an index), write one row for the lowest-numbered port and list the others in "Same Role", each by its exact closed-set name; never shorten that list.
   Each port in the conceptual asset's "Enters Through" and "Leaves Through" lists is a structural asset of it: name it at the point that reads or writes it, even when every answer there is "no"; step 4 labels it by 2.6.
   Each element named here is a structural asset of this conceptual asset. If the conceptual asset passes from a port to a port with no point between them, say so in "Notes".

4. Primary. Among the structural assets of step 3, label primary each element that 2.6 makes primary for this conceptual asset: where its value is stored or computed in this unit, where it crosses the unit's boundary whole, or, for a decision or setting, the element that holds or makes it. A non-record unit port that moves the conceptual asset's value one bit per step crosses the boundary whole, as every bit passes it: the RTL shifts the port's bit into one end of a register carrying that value (2.2), or drives the port from such a register's end bit, even through forwarding wires, synchronizer stages or one-bit registers, and even if the register holds other fields or the port carries other bits at other steps. Label the port primary, not secondary as a path to the register, which keeps its own label. A serial clock line carries no bit of the value, so it is never such a port. An output port set only from a stored selection, one line per value (a decode), returns it unmixed: label it primary for that selection. Each bar in this step (saying an element never qualifies or is not primary) holds even when CONCEPTS names the element, cites its lines as held lines or lists it under "Enters Through" or "Leaves Through", and even where 2.6, 2.8 or another sentence would make it primary or 4.4 points at it; a bar worded "for any conceptual asset" also covers one about the element itself. A barred element can still be a secondary (2.7). What a rule judges as usual or does not cover is spared only from that rule's bar. A bus request or response record port, or any field of one, never qualifies (2.6), even when it is the only port the conceptual asset enters or leaves through. An internal array whose elements this unit sets each to a whole bus request or response record (2.6), copied from one record or a constant, one element per port, never qualifies for any conceptual asset either: it only passes the transaction on, and a select or compare gating an element's strobe only decides that field (2.2) or is mixed into it (2.6), so the decision stays where it is computed. A select signal computed from that compare (such as a vector with a bit per port) is the decision's own element and stays its primary. If the compare is inline in a condition, the decision has no element of its own and adds no primary, even when the held lines assign the array; do not move the primary to the record the array copies or its fields, which only feed the compare or carry the transaction. A single strobe, enable or request flag that a decision gates is judged as usual. A record this unit only wires between one of its ports and the port map of an instance of another IP, with no statement of this unit reading or writing it or its fields (a constant tied to it in a generate branch lacking the instance is no such statement), never qualifies for any conceptual asset, nor do its fields: the instance holds the value, and the record only carries it. Judged as usual: a record a statement of this unit reads or writes, a non-record port even if wired straight into an instance, an element of this unit deciding whether or where the record passes, and, in a unit executing instructions, a record carrying the instructions it executes or the data it loads and stores, though a bus request or response record port still never qualifies. A setting or decision whose only use, apart from a read-back, is to shape the clock line of a serial link the unit drives to, or senses from, a peer device (its polarity, its phase against the data, or holding it while the peer stretches it) never qualifies for any conceptual asset either: Phase 1 does not keep it as one. Judged as usual: the link's data and select lines, its rate setting (how often the line toggles), an enable, and the unit's clock-enable outputs. When a bus read returns, unchanged, words that some build (one generate branch) holds only in a ROM, which is no element (section 1), the element the ROM lookup writes in that build (a ROM's read data) is where they are stored or computed in this unit (2.6), not a carrier, as no element before it holds them: label it primary for their conceptual asset, even where another build only passes a stored word through it, since each pair gets one label. A bus response field the lookup writes directly still never qualifies. In a cache (a unit answering a read from its own copy when a stored tag matches the address, else fetching the words over a backing bus), the copied words only copy what the backing memory holds, and the tag and valid bits only record which words are copied, so the arrays holding them, and any signal or port only carrying their contents in or out, never qualify for any conceptual asset. Judged as usual: the cache's decisions (the hit, the bytes a write changes, the address, its fill, clear and invalidate commands, a pending fence or flush) and a memory that is itself the store. If none qualifies for a conceptual asset, give the reason in its "No Primary Reason". Any other element that 2.6 says is not primary never qualifies either, even where this step's clause on a decision or setting would make it one. A reset request that this unit only combines, gates, registers or passes on to a reset chain or reset output port never qualifies for any conceptual asset, nor do the input port bringing it in, its copies, that output port, or a chain only synchronizing or releasing a reset (a reset shift register): they only carry a decision judged where it is computed. Judged as usual: the record of a reset's cause, a compare outcome that restarts a counter, and, except as the next sentence says, a decision triggering a reset (such as a timeout), where it is computed. A decision that a write was refused or carried a wrong value (a write while locked, a wrong password), its gated or registered copies, and a setting choosing only whether such a violation resets never qualify for any conceptual asset if their only uses, apart from a read-back, are to request a reset and record its cause. Judged as usual: the cause record, the lock refusing the write, a compare outcome that restarts a counter, a decision triggering a reset for another cause (such as a timeout) where it is computed, and a refused access raising a trap, a fault or a bus error. A tick, edge pulse or divider count made or picked inside the unit only to pace a step (apart from restarting itself, it only makes such a pulse or lets the step advance) is not primary for any conceptual asset unless an output port carries the pulse itself (2.2); a port the pulses only decide or time does not count. It can be a secondary only of a primary whose update it gates (2.7, direction b). Judged as usual: the element storing the rate setting, a request held until served, a start pulse decoded from a received command, a step counter, and a count whose compare with a limit raises a reset, an error or an interrupt, or sets an output level (a timeout or timer count). A register only recording that a count has started, read only to let that count advance or to qualify a compare on it, is not primary for any conceptual asset: it decides the count but never carries it (2.2). It can be a secondary only in direction b (2.7). Judged as usual: the compare's result, a flag a request or command sets until served (a busy or pending flag), a start or clear pulse decoded from a command, a timeout's suppression flag, and a completion flag. Partial results inside a computation of this unit are part of the result it builds, so they never qualify for any conceptual asset: a partial sum or product, an addend, a subtraction step or round value, a value before the final sign step, a value computed only to be copied, whole or in part, into the finished result's register, a compare only picking that result (a minimum or maximum), and the state and flag a debiasing step makes to choose which raw bits it accepts. Judged as usual: the element holding the finished result or that an output port takes it from, even while it holds operand copies or partial values; a unit port; a signal handed whole to or from an instance; a count; a setting (such as signedness); a serial port's data shift register; and any other decision, such as a compare whose result picks or triggers what happens next, or checks combined per region or entry. A synchronizer stage is not primary for any conceptual asset: a register whose assignments, apart from constants, only copy in one unit input port's bit (directly or through a wire or type conversion only copying it) and shift its own earlier bits along; a wire only copying one of its bits is part of it. Judged as usual: the port itself; a register copying a port with none of its own earlier bits (an input register software reads back); a register whose new bit comes from a stage (a shift register assembling a received word); and an element mixing the port or a stage's bits with other values (a request held until served, a sticky flag). In a unit executing instructions, when a conceptual asset is the word an instance outputs for reads of its control and status registers, which a statement of this unit merges directly into the value written back to a register, the read words other instances pass into that instance, and any word merging them (an OR), never qualify for that conceptual asset: that read-back is held at the instance output, and they carry other instances' registers. Judge from port names which instance output and inputs these are, and say so. Judged as usual: that instance output, and these words under other conceptual assets. If a FIFO instance's write data is a unit input port's value (a bus write, or bits shifted in from a serial input port), the FIFO only buffers data between two ports, so its fill flags (whether it holds data or has room), push and pop strobes, a command only clearing it, and a flag recording a push while full only time, gate, clear or report on that data: they never qualify for any conceptual asset, and barring them makes no other element primary in their place. Judged as usual: the buffered data, an output port these flags decide (an interrupt request, a ready-to-receive line), a unit enable that also clears the FIFO, and the flags and controls of a FIFO holding data the unit generates itself (random bits). Judge from port names that an instance is a FIFO and which outputs are its flags, and say so. In a unit shifting data bit by bit onto or off a serial data port, paced by its own rate setting, the engine doing it only decides when that data moves: its state register, bit and phase counters, and the busy and done flags made from them never qualify for any conceptual asset, and where 2.6 would make only these primary, the conceptual asset has none. They can be secondaries only in direction b (2.7). Judged as usual: the data, chip-select and flow-control ports, an interrupt request these flags decide, the enable and rate settings, and the data shift register. Not covered (no such port or rate setting): a unit computing bit by bit from parallel operands, one shifting in bits it generates, and a test or debug access port the peer's clock paces. In a unit executing an instruction or atomic memory operation a processor issues to it (a coprocessor, an atomic read-modify-write), the instruction or request fields only picking which operation runs or which result form returns (an opcode, a function code, an instruction-type bit), a stored copy of them, and a flag decoded from them only naming the operation never qualify for any conceptual asset: they are part of the operation they pick, not a decision of their own. They can be secondaries only in direction b (2.7). A flag whose assignment also reads anything besides these fields (a start or valid signal, a state) does not only name the operation. Judged as usual: a start command or pulse, even one decoded per operation (a multiply start, a divide start), a register access's address and write enable, a completion flag, and a setting decoded from these fields (not a copy) that changes how operands are read (such as signedness). A plain bus read or write is not covered: its read or write bit and a read enable made from it stay judged as usual. A register only remembering a request's own lock flag while that locked sequence lasts, or the previous grant for round-robin priority, and a signal used only to compute such a register's next value never qualify for any conceptual asset, and one about that flag alone has no primary: the requester sets the flag anew with each request, and the previous grant only tilts the next one, so the register holds no decision of its own. It can be a secondary only of the primaries of the grant or transfer monitor that reads it (2.7, direction b). Judged as usual: the current grant, its select and strobe, requests held until served, a reservation, a lock software sets to guard later accesses, and a timeout count with its suppression flag.

5. Secondary. For each primary, check the other structural assets of step 3, and the primary's direct counterparts in the closed set, its "clocked by" and "reset by" partners included. Take a "gets value from" or "gives value to" partner when its cited lines are lines where the primary carries this conceptual asset. Take a "decided by" or "decides" partner when the test on its cited line decides such a line; that cited line is the test, which is usually not the line it decides. Take a "clocked by" or "reset by" partner of the primary when an attacker could plausibly use it to stall, block or clear the primary (2.7, direction b). Also take the "decided by" partners of each structural asset that carries the primary's value out of the unit, at the lines where it carries this conceptual asset: they decide the release (2.7, direction b), and they include the deciding unit ports that step 3 leaves out. When an instance of another IP sets the primary, the closed set shows only the instance's own port on that side: then also check the elements wired into that instance's other inputs, citing the port-map lines, and name the instance in its "Justification". Label secondary each one that meets 2.7, and list under its "Secondary To" each primary it serves: that primary's name and entity, and the direction (a or b). A direction counts only for an objective the conceptual asset has (2.7). A structural asset of step 3 that is neither primary nor secondary is not reported in "Assets"; list it under "Not Reported" with the reason. A conceptual asset with no primary has no secondary either, as a secondary names a primary under "Secondary To" (2.7): list all its structural assets under "Not Reported", its "Enters Through" and "Leaves Through" ports included.

Rules:
- List every point you reach in "Points", including the points where every answer is "no", so that each step of the walk can be checked against the arrows you followed.
- Report each (element, conceptual asset) pair once. An element can appear under several conceptual assets, with a different label for each.
- Report only closed-set elements, under the constraints of section 1.
